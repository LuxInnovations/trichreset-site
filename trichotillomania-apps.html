Updated 14 October 2026 · By the Trich Reset team · Every claim links to its source below

The short answer

There are four main types of trichotillomania app: therapist platforms (TrichStop), wearables that buzz when your hand moves (HabitAware Keen2), trackers with a habit reversal course (SkinAware), and audio-first apps for the urge itself (Trich Reset). They suit different people. This page is written by the team behind Trich Reset, so we have stuck to facts you can check and linked our source for other apps' prices.[1]

What makes a good trich app

  • Built on habit reversal training. It is the best-evidenced approach. See treatments compared.
  • Fast to use. If logging takes a minute, you will stop by week two.
  • Doesn't punish a bad day. Shame keeps the cycle going. Streaks that reset to zero can make things worse.
  • Private. You should be able to delete everything yourself.
  • Honest about what it is. No app is a medical treatment. Be wary of "cure" claims.

Common questions

What is the best app for trichotillomania?

It depends on how you pull. TrichStop connects you with a specialist therapist (about US$60–90 a week). HabitAware Keen2 is a bracelet that detects automatic pulling. SkinAware offers detailed logging and a habit reversal course with a free tier. Trich Reset (Android) focuses on guided audio for the moment an urge arrives, plus tracking and lessons.

Is there a free app for hair pulling?

SkinAware has a free core logging tier, and the HabitAware app is free for manual logging. Trich Reset offers one full meditation free on its website. Fully featured apps generally need a subscription.

Do trichotillomania apps work?

Most apps use principles from habit reversal training, which has strong evidence as a therapy. Few apps have been tested in their own clinical trials, so treat any app as support rather than treatment.

Not yet. Trich Reset is on Android through Google Play, with iOS planned.
