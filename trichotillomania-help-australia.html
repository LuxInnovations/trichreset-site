Trichotillomania help in Australia

How to get help for hair pulling in Australia: a GP mental health treatment plan, up to 10 Medicare-rebated psychology sessions a year, finding a BFRB-trained psychologist, and who to call.

Updated 18 October 2026 · By the Trich Reset team · Every claim links to its source below

The short answer

In Australia, start with your GP. They can write a mental health treatment plan and refer you to a psychologist, and Medicare then pays part of the cost of up to 10 individual sessions (and 10 group sessions) each calendar year.[1][2] The first referral is for up to 6 sessions, then your GP reviews and can refer you for more.[1] Ask for a psychologist who uses habit reversal training, the treatment with the strongest evidence for trichotillomania.[3]

What it costs

Medicare pays a rebate for each eligible session, and many psychologists charge more than the rebate, so expect a gap fee. It varies a lot between practices, so ask when you book. Some bulk bill, especially for students, concession card holders or children.

Finding a psychologist who knows trich

BFRB specialists are thin on the ground in Australia, so widen the search:

  • The TLC Foundation directory at bfrb.org lists clinicians trained in body-focused repetitive behaviours, including some outside the US.[4]
  • The Australian Psychological Society's Find a Psychologist at findapsychologist.org.au. Search for "habit reversal", "BFRB" or "OCD and related disorders".
  • Video sessions mean the right psychologist doesn't have to be in your city.[1]
  • If you can't find a trich specialist, a psychologist experienced with OCD and related disorders and habit reversal training is a sound second choice.

Common questions

Does Medicare cover a psychologist for trichotillomania?

Yes. With a mental health treatment plan from your GP, Medicare pays a rebate for up to 10 individual and 10 group sessions with an eligible psychologist, occupational therapist or social worker each calendar year. Many practices charge a gap on top.

How do I get a mental health treatment plan?

Book a long appointment with your GP and say it is for a mental health treatment plan. Your GP assesses you, writes the plan and refers you for up to 6 sessions, then reviews you and can refer you for more.

How do I find a trichotillomania specialist in Australia?

Check the TLC Foundation directory at bfrb.org and the Australian Psychological Society's Find a Psychologist. Ask whether they use habit reversal training or the ComB model. Video sessions are claimable, so the psychologist does not have to be local.

Can I see a dermatologist for hair pulling?

A dermatologist can rule out other causes of hair loss and help with the skin, but trichotillomania is treated with behaviour therapy, so you will also need a psychologist. Tell your GP it is hair pulling, not only hair loss.
