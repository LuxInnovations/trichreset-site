Trichotillomania in children

Is your child pulling out their hair? What trichotillomania looks like at each age, what helps and what makes it worse, and how behaviour therapy helped 75% of children in a trial.

Updated 18 October 2026 · By the Trich Reset team · Every claim links to its source below

The short answer

Trichotillomania often starts in late childhood or early puberty,[1] and children respond well to treatment. In a randomised trial of children aged 7 to 17, 75% responded to eight weeks of behaviour therapy, compared with 0% in the comparison group, and 7 to 9-year-olds did as well as the older children.[2] The most useful thing a parent can be is a calm coach, not the hair police.[3]

What helps, and what makes it worse

For parents: do and don't
Instead of…Try…

These follow habit reversal training, the best-evidenced treatment,[4] which the children's trial adapted for kids.[2] The practical guide explains each step, and the trigger table there works well as a family worksheet.

One thing to watch for

Some children chew or swallow the hair they pull. Over time this can form a hairball in the stomach (a trichobezoar), which may need surgery.[1] If your child eats hair and has stomach pain, nausea, vomiting or a poor appetite, see a doctor.

Getting help

Start with your GP or paediatrician, and say "trichotillomania". Ask for a psychologist experienced with children and habit reversal training. In Australia, Medicare rebates up to 10 sessions a year with a mental health treatment plan, and a paediatrician can refer. Parents can join sessions as part of their child's plan.[5] See trichotillomania help in Australia for the steps, and the TLC Foundation directory at bfrb.org to find a BFRB-trained clinician.

Common questions

Why is my child pulling out their hair?

Hair pulling in children is usually linked to soothing, boredom, tiredness or stress, and often happens while falling asleep, reading or watching a screen. When it is repeated and hard to stop, it may be trichotillomania, which commonly starts in late childhood or early puberty.

Will my child grow out of trichotillomania?

Some very young children ("baby trich") do, but many do not without help. The good news is that children respond well to behaviour therapy: in one trial, 75% of children aged 7 to 17 responded after eight weeks.

Should I tell my child to stop pulling?

Telling them to stop, punishing or checking their hair tends to add shame, which can make pulling worse. Agree a quiet signal together, praise them for using strategies, and change the setup, such as tying hair back at bedtime.

What kind of doctor treats trichotillomania in children?

Start with your GP or paediatrician. Treatment is behaviour therapy with a psychologist, ideally one experienced with children and habit reversal training. The TLC Foundation lists BFRB clinicians at bfrb.org.
